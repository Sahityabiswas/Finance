1. Problem Statement
Commercial banks and Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs) are two important parts of India's financial
sector. Although both provide financial services and credit, they operate with different business models and may
respond differently to changes in market conditions.
From an investor's point of view, it is useful to understand which segment provides better returns and how much
risk an investor has to take to achieve those returns. It is also important to know whether investing in both banks
and NBFCs together can reduce overall portfolio risk.
This project therefore focuses on comparing selected Indian commercial banks and NBFCs using historical
stock-market data. The study will examine their returns, volatility and risk-adjusted performance and will then
compare simple portfolios made from the two groups. A basic option-pricing analysis will also be included to
understand how the risk of selected financial-sector stocks is reflected in the derivatives market.
2. Abstract
This project aims to study and compare the risk and return characteristics of selected commercial banks and
NBFCs in India. Historical stock-price data will be used to calculate returns, volatility and basic risk-adjusted
measures for companies from both sectors.
The study will first examine the individual performance of selected banks and NBFCs and identify which group has
historically provided better returns relative to its risk. The correlation between stocks will then be studied to
understand whether combining banks and NBFCs can provide diversification benefits. Simple portfolio
combinations will be compared to determine whether a mixed portfolio can achieve a better balance between risk
and return.
As an additional part of the study, selected equity options will be analyzed using the Black-Scholes model. The
theoretical option prices will be compared with market prices, and selected option Greeks will be calculated to
understand the effect of stock price and volatility on option values.
The overall aim is to provide a simple quantitative comparison of banks and NBFCs from an investor's perspective
without making the project unnecessarily complex.

4. Methodology
Step 1 – Data Collection and Cleaning: Historical stock-price data will be collected from NSE and cleaned before
analysis. Missing observations and inconsistent records will be handled appropriately.
Step 2 – Return Calculation: Daily stock returns will be calculated from historical prices. Average returns will then
be compared across the selected banks and NBFCs.
Step 3 – Risk Analysis: The main risk measure will be volatility, calculated using the standard deviation of returns.
Maximum drawdown, Sharpe ratio and beta will also be considered.
Step 4 – Bank vs NBFC Comparison: Average return and volatility will be compared using tables and visualizations
to identify differences in performance and risk.
Step 5 – Simple Portfolio Analysis: Three portfolios will be constructed: a bank-only portfolio, an NBFC-only
portfolio, and a 50:50 Bank-NBFC portfolio. Their return, volatility and Sharpe ratio will be compared.
Step 6 – Basic Option Analysis: For selected stocks with actively traded options, the Black-Scholes model will be
used to calculate theoretical option prices. These will be compared with observed market prices, and selected
Greeks such as Delta, Gamma and Vega will be calculated.
5. Objectives
Main Objective: To compare the risk and return characteristics of selected Indian commercial banks and NBFCs
and examine whether combining the two sectors provides diversification benefits to investors.
1.
To calculate and compare the historical returns of selected commercial banks and NBFCs.
2.
To compare the volatility and risk associated with the two groups.
3.
To evaluate the risk-adjusted performance of selected stocks using the Sharpe ratio and beta.
4.
To determine whether NBFCs provide higher returns at the cost of higher risk compared with commercial
banks.

7. Expected Outcome
The project is expected to provide a clear comparison of the return and risk characteristics of commercial banks
and NBFCs. It will also show whether combining the two sectors can improve portfolio diversification. The option
analysis will provide an additional perspective on how market participants price risk in selected financial-sector
stocks.
